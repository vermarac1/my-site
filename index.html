The symptom

You know the symptom. The problem is usually somewhere else.

  • “Leads are down.”Maybe it isn't demand. The market may not understand what you sell.
  • “Deals aren't converting.”Maybe it's positioning. If buyers can't tell why you, they benchmark on price.
  • “Sales says the leads are weak.”Maybe it's qualification. Two definitions of a lead create one argument.
  • “CAC is climbing.”Maybe it's the ICP. A thin list costs more to convert.
  • “Marketing is busy.”Maybe there is no system. Activity without one produces more activity.

Market Shyam Spectra · NaaS and enterprise connectivity From consumer broadband to enterprise B2B 45% lead generation20% sales conversion40% retention7,000+ leads managed

The problem

A consumer broadband business had to sell to enterprise buyers, with no enterprise positioning, ICP, demand engine or shared qualification with sales.

The constraint

Market. Nobody could say which enterprise accounts to win, so channel, message and sales coverage ran on assumption.

What I did

  • Defined the ICP from conversion data and matched every CRM lead to ICP and persona
  • Rebuilt enterprise positioning and launched three flagship products
  • Built a multi-channel GTM motion and automated sales handover in HubSpot
  • Sold Wi-Fi Pro myself, in the field, to paying guest operators in Bangalore

The lesson

Once you have personally sold the thing, sales stops seeing you as the person who sends leads.

Message BlueKaktus · AI supply chain and enterprise software Repositioning an ERP company as an AI supply chain platform 3 enterprise verticals15+ sales and launch assets6 months zero to running process

The problem

The company was positioned as ERP but needed to compete as an AI supply chain platform, and sales had no case studies, battle cards or enablement.

The constraint

Message. The category was wrong, and the positioning had to work for brands and manufacturers at once.

What I did

  • Built the positioning and messaging architecture for both sides of the market
  • Wrote the website and aligned founders, product and sales to a CEO deadline
  • Went live in time for the CEO's Netherlands meeting
  • Built enablement from nothing: case studies, battle cards, decks and a fortnightly sales cadence

The lesson

A senior marketer should be able to say they decided what the company says to the market, not only that leads went up.

Motion NayaOne · FinTech SaaS, United Kingdom Refilling a dry enterprise funnel with £3,000 £3,000 total spend50 intent-qualified prospects35% lead to conversion2 months to funnel movement

The problem

The enterprise funnel had gone quiet. The market was small and risk-sensitive, so more paid reach would have bought impressions, not buyers.

The constraint

Motion. Buyers signalling intent existed; the channel we paid for wasn't reaching them.

What I did

  • Chose content syndication over more paid spend and negotiated directly with TechTarget
  • Got the £3,000 programme signed off by the CEO and wrote the white paper myself
  • Structured the terms around demonstrated intent, not impressions
  • Routed returned names straight to inside sales, with context

The lesson

In a small enterprise market you need better access to buyers already signalling intent, not more reach.

Measurement AgreeYa Solutions · Enterprise IT and SaaS, US market Marketing built around a $250K demand mandate ~$1M revenue contribution1,000+ qualified leads a quarter50% lead quality30% conversion10,000+ visitors in quarter one

The problem

Growth ran on events and relationships. Two acquired products needed a digital demand engine, with no funnel, acquisition model or shared lead definition.

The constraint

Measurement. Without funnel maths nothing marketing did connected to revenue, so the budget was a matter of faith.

What I did

  • Worked back from the revenue target to opportunities, SQLs, MQLs, channels and budget
  • Defined every funnel stage with sales, signed off by sales and the CEO
  • Tied marketing's target to revenue, not lead volume
  • Built multi-channel acquisition and the attribution layer from the start

The lesson

Sales accepts your definitions once you accept being measured on the outcome.

Point of view

I don't start with channels.

  1. 01

    Start with the business problem.

    The objective, the buyer and the number that has to move come before any channel.

  2. 02

    Position before promotion.

    If the market doesn't understand the proposition, more media creates more noise.

  3. 03

    Build the funnel as a system.

    Acquisition, nurture, conversion and handover are connected. Optimising one rarely fixes the business.

  4. 04

    One language for marketing and sales.

    ICP, MQL and SQL must mean the same thing to both teams, or the funnel maths isn't real.

  5. 05

    Data informs judgement.

    Analytics shows where the system breaks. Judgement decides what to investigate.

  6. 06

    AI should increase leverage.

    I use it for research and content velocity, not strategy. It makes it easy to produce more of what isn't working.
